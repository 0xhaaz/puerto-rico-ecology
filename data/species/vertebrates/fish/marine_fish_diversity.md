# Marine Fish Diversity of Borikén (Puerto Rico)

The waters surrounding Borikén are home to over 803 fish species, making these Caribbean waters one of the most biodiverse marine ecosystems in the Atlantic. From the coral reefs that fringe the coasts to the deep ocean trenches, these waters have sustained Taíno communities for millennia and continue to support contemporary Boricua fishing traditions and marine ecosystems.

## Endemic and Near-Endemic Species

### Euripos Jewelfish
**Scientific Name**: *Odontanthias hensleyi*  
**Status**: Endemic to Puerto Rico  
**Habitat**: Deep reefs (300-1000 feet)

A stunning deep-water fish known only from Puerto Rican waters.

**Cultural Significance:**
- Recently discovered species representing unknown biodiversity
- Symbol of the mysteries still hidden in Puerto Rican waters
- Demonstrates the importance of protecting deep-water habitats

### Opalescent Grunt
**Scientific Name**: *Rhonciscus pauco*  
**Status**: Endemic to Puerto Rico  
**Habitat**: Deep coral reefs

### Puerto Rican Snake Eel
**Scientific Name**: *Bascanichthys inopinatus*  
**Status**: Endemic  
**Habitat**: Sandy bottoms and seagrass beds

### Akarnax Slopefish
**Scientific Name**: *Symphysanodon mona*  
**Status**: Endemic, known from Mona Passage  
**Habitat**: Deep slopes

## Culturally Significant Food Fish

### Dorado / Mahi-mahi
**Scientific Name**: *Coryphaena hippurus*  
**Taíno Name**: Dorado  
**Cultural Importance**: High - traditional fishing target

The Dorado has been central to Caribbean fishing culture for centuries.

**Cultural Significance:**
- Traditional target of Taíno fishers using sophisticated techniques
- Featured in fishing festivals and cultural celebrations
- Symbol of abundance and successful fishing
- Important in traditional cuisine and family gatherings

**Ecological Role:**
- Pelagic predator important for open ocean food webs
- Indicator of healthy offshore ecosystems
- Migratory species connecting Caribbean waters

**Traditional Fishing Methods:**
- Line fishing from traditional canoes
- Seasonal timing based on traditional knowledge
- Sustainable practices ensuring population continuity

### Chillo / Red Snapper
**Scientific Name**: *Lutjanus purpureus*  
**Traditional Importance**: Extremely high  
**Habitat**: Coral reefs and rocky bottoms

One of the most prized fish in Puerto Rican cuisine and fishing tradition.

**Cultural Significance:**
- Central to Puerto Rican fishing heritage
- Featured in traditional recipes and celebrations
- Symbol of fishing skill and community prosperity
- Important in local economics and food security

**Traditional Knowledge:**
- Seasonal migration patterns known for generations
- Traditional fishing spots passed down through families
- Sustainable harvesting practices based on traditional calendar

### Pargo / Lane Snapper
**Scientific Name**: *Lutjanus synagris*  
**Status**: Important food fish  
**Traditional Name**: Pargo Colorado

### Sama / Silk Snapper
**Scientific Name**: *Lutjanus vivanus*  
**Status**: Deep water snapper  
**Cultural Importance**: Traditional deep-water fishing target

## Reef Fish Communities

### Parrotfish / Cotorras del Mar
Multiple species including:
- **Blue Parrotfish** (*Scarus coeruleus*) - Cotorra Azul
- **Stoplight Parrotfish** (*Sparisoma viride*) - Cotorra Colorada
- **Princess Parrotfish** (*Scarus taeniopterus*) - Cotorra Princesa

**Cultural Significance:**
- Associated with coral reef health in traditional knowledge
- Colorful species featured in art and decoration
- Traditional understanding of their role in sand production

**Ecological Role:**
- Critical for coral reef health through algae grazing
- Produce sand through coral consumption and processing
- Indicator species for reef ecosystem health

### Angelfish / Peces Ángel
- **French Angelfish** (*Pomacanthus paru*) - Ángel Francés
- **Gray Angelfish** (*Pomacanthus arcuatus*) - Ángel Gris
- **Queen Angelfish** (*Holacanthus ciliaris*) - Ángel Reina

**Cultural Significance:**
- Associated with spiritual protection in some traditions
- Popular in marine aquarium trade
- Symbol of reef beauty and diversity

### Wrasses / Doncella
- **Spanish Hogfish** (*Bodianus rufus*) - Doncella
- **Yellowhead Wrasse** (*Halichoeres garnoti*) - Doncella Amarilla
- **Puddingwife** (*Halichoeres radiatus*) - Doncella Rayada

**Ecological Role:**
- Important cleaners removing parasites from other fish
- Control of small invertebrate populations

## Traditional Fishing Knowledge

### Seasonal Patterns
- **Fish Calendars**: Traditional knowledge of when different species are most abundant
- **Weather Patterns**: Understanding how weather affects fish behavior
- **Moon Phases**: Traditional timing of fishing based on lunar cycles
- **Current Patterns**: Knowledge of how water movement affects fish locations

### Traditional Fishing Methods
- **Line Fishing**: From shore and traditional canoes
- **Net Fishing**: Various types for different species and habitats
- **Trap Fishing**: Fixed traps in traditional locations
- **Spear Fishing**: Free diving with traditional spears

### Sustainable Practices
- **Seasonal Closures**: Traditional periods when certain species were not harvested
- **Size Restrictions**: Traditional minimum sizes based on life cycle knowledge
- **Habitat Protection**: Recognition of important spawning and nursery areas
- **Community Management**: Traditional systems for managing fishing access

## Modern Conservation Challenges

### Major Threats
1. **Overfishing**: Both commercial and recreational
2. **Habitat Destruction**: Coastal development affecting nursery areas
3. **Pollution**: Agricultural runoff and coastal pollution
4. **Climate Change**: Ocean warming and acidification
5. **Invasive Species**: Lionfish and other non-native species
6. **Coral Reef Degradation**: Loss of essential fish habitat

### Conservation Efforts
- **Marine Protected Areas**: No-take zones allowing fish populations to recover
- **Fishing Regulations**: Size and season restrictions based on science and tradition
- **Habitat Restoration**: Coral reef and mangrove restoration projects
- **Community Education**: Teaching sustainable fishing practices
- **Research Programs**: Monitoring fish populations and ecosystem health

### Traditional Knowledge Integration
- **Fishers' Knowledge**: Incorporating traditional observations into management
- **Community-Based Management**: Local participation in conservation decisions
- **Traditional Practices**: Reviving sustainable traditional fishing methods
- **Cultural Education**: Teaching young people about fishing heritage

## Ecological Relationships

### Food Web Dynamics
- **Primary Consumers**: Herbivorous fish maintaining algae balance
- **Secondary Consumers**: Planktivores and small predators
- **Apex Predators**: Sharks, large groupers, and barracuda
- **Cleaners**: Wrasses and gobies providing cleaning services

### Habitat Dependencies
- **Coral Reefs**: Complex three-dimensional habitat supporting high diversity
- **Seagrass Beds**: Nursery areas for many commercially important species
- **Mangroves**: Critical nursery habitat for coastal and reef species
- **Open Ocean**: Migratory pathways and feeding areas for large pelagics

### Symbiotic Relationships
- **Cleaner Fish**: Removing parasites from larger fish
- **Anemone Fish**: Living within sea anemone protection
- **Goby-Shrimp Partnerships**: Shared burrows and mutual protection
