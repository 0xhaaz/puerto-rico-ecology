# Endemic Reptiles of Borikén (Puerto Rico)

Borikén hosts 77 reptile species, with an extraordinary 46 species (59.7%) being endemic—one of the highest rates of endemism in the Caribbean. These reptiles have evolved unique adaptations to the island's diverse habitats and hold significant places in Taíno culture and contemporary Boricua identity.

## Iconic Large Species

### Culebra / Puerto Rican Boa
**Scientific Name**: *Chilabothrus inornatus*  
**Taíno Name**: Majá  
**Conservation Status**: Vulnerable  
**Size**: Up to 6.5 feet (2 meters)

The Puerto Rican Boa is the island's largest native predator and apex reptile, playing a crucial ecological role in controlling rodent populations.

**Cultural Significance:**
- Sacred serpent in Taíno mythology, associated with water spirits and fertility
- Featured prominently in petroglyphs throughout the island
- Symbol of ancient wisdom and connection to earth energies
- Contemporary symbol of wildness and natural heritage

**Ecological Role:**
- Controls populations of rats and other small mammals
- Important predator in cave ecosystems where it hunts bats
- Indicator species for healthy forest ecosystems

**Traditional Knowledge:**
- Taíno people never killed these snakes, considering them protectors
- Associated with rainfall and agricultural fertility
- Believed to guard sacred caves and water sources

**Current Threats:**
- Habitat destruction and road mortality
- Human persecution due to fear and misunderstanding
- Invasive prey species disrupting natural food webs

### Iguana Terrestre / Mona Ground Iguana
**Scientific Name**: *Cyclura cornuta stejnegeri*  
**Conservation Status**: Critically Endangered  
**Location**: Mona Island only

This massive iguana, endemic to Mona Island, can reach 4 feet in length and represents one of the Caribbean's most endangered reptiles.

**Cultural Significance:**
- Associated with the sun and earth in Taíno beliefs
- Symbol of strength and endurance
- Mona Island considered sacred by indigenous peoples

**Ecological Role:**
- Primary herbivore on Mona Island
- Important seed disperser for native plants
- Ecosystem engineer, creating burrows used by other species

## Diverse Anole Communities

Borikén hosts 11 endemic anole species, representing one of the most spectacular adaptive radiations in the Caribbean.

### Lagartijo Gigante / Puerto Rican Giant Anole
**Scientific Name**: *Anolis cuvieri*  
**Conservation Status**: Vulnerable  
**Size**: Up to 7 inches body length

The largest anole in the Caribbean, this impressive lizard is found in mature forests.

**Cultural Significance:**
- Associated with forest spirits and ancient wisdom
- Symbol of the island's unique evolutionary heritage

**Ecological Role:**
- Top predator among anoles, controls smaller lizard populations
- Important part of forest food webs
- Indicator of mature forest health

### Lagartijo Verde / Green Anole
**Scientific Name**: *Anolis evermanni*  
**Status**: Endemic, common in appropriate habitat

**Cultural Significance:**
- Symbol of adaptation and resilience
- Featured in children's stories and folklore

### Lagartijo de Hierba / Grass Anole
**Scientific Name**: *Anolis pulchellus*  
**Status**: Endemic, stable

### Lagartijo Manchado / Spotted Anole
**Scientific Name**: *Anolis stratulus*  
**Status**: Endemic, stable

### Lagartijo de Tronco / Bark Anole
**Scientific Name**: *Anolis gundlachi*  
**Status**: Endemic, stable

### Other Endemic Anoles:
- *Anolis occultus* (Dwarf/Twig Anole)
- *Anolis cooki* (Cook's Anole) - Guánica dry forest
- *Anolis poncensis* (Ponce Anole)
- *Anolis krugi* (Krug's Anole)
- *Anolis cristatellus* (Crested Anole) - though some consider this native rather than endemic
- *Anolis monensis* (Mona Anole) - Mona Island

## Ground Dwellers and Burrowers

### Lagarto Azul / Puerto Rican Blue-tailed Ameiva
**Scientific Name**: *Ameiva exsul*  
**Status**: Endemic, stable

A fast-moving ground lizard with striking blue tail coloration in juveniles.

**Cultural Significance:**
- Associated with speed and agility in traditional stories
- Blue tail considered a good luck symbol

**Ecological Role:**
- Important insect predator
- Prey for larger reptiles and birds
- Indicator of healthy ground-level ecosystems

### Culebrita Ciega / Puerto Rican Blindsnake
**Scientific Name**: *Typhlops platycephalus*  
**Status**: Endemic, poorly known

This small, worm-like snake lives underground and is rarely seen.

**Ecological Role:**
- Controls ant and termite populations
- Important decomposer in soil ecosystems

## Gecko Diversity

Borikén hosts several endemic gecko species, including some of the Caribbean's smallest reptiles.

### Salamanquita / Dwarf Gecko species
Multiple *Sphaerodactylus* species including:
- *S. grandisquamis* (Puerto Rican Dwarf Gecko)
- *S. klauberi* (Upland Gecko)
- *S. roosevelti* (Roosevelt's Gecko)
- *S. townsendi* (Townsend's Gecko)
- *S. inigoi* (Vieques Dwarf Gecko)

**Cultural Significance:**
- Associated with house spirits and protection in folk beliefs
- Considered beneficial for controlling insects

## Unique Adaptations

### Culebrilla de Cristal / Puerto Rican Galliwasp
**Scientific Name**: *Diploglossus pleei*  
**Status**: Endemic, rare

A legless lizard that resembles a snake but is actually more closely related to typical lizards.

**Cultural Significance:**
- Associated with transformation and adaptation
- Symbol of the island's evolutionary uniqueness

### Corredora / Puerto Rican Racer
**Scientific Name**: *Borikenophis portoricensis*  
**Status**: Endemic, stable

A fast-moving, non-venomous snake important for rodent control.

**Ecological Role:**
- Controls rodent populations in agricultural areas
- Important prey for the Puerto Rican Boa

## Island Endemics

### Especies de Mona / Mona Island Species
- **Mona Boa** (*Chilabothrus monensis*)
- **Mona Dwarf Gecko** (*Sphaerodactylus monensis*)
- **Mona Anole** (*Anolis monensis*)

### Especies de Culebra / Culebra Island Species
- **Culebra Island Giant Anole** (*Anolis roosevelti*) - Possibly extinct
- **Culebra Ameiva** (*Ameiva alboguttata*)

## Conservation Status and Threats

### Major Threats
1. **Habitat Loss**: Urban development and agriculture
2. **Invasive Species**: Cats, mongoose, cane toads
3. **Road Mortality**: Particularly affecting boas and large lizards
4. **Climate Change**: Sea level rise affecting coastal species
5. **Collection**: Illegal pet trade

### Conservation Successes
- Protected habitat designation in El Yunque and Guánica
- Captive breeding programs for critically endangered species
- Community education programs
- Research and monitoring initiatives

### Traditional Conservation Wisdom
- Taíno practices of seasonal habitat protection
- Traditional knowledge of species behavior and ecology
- Sacred site protection preserving critical habitats
- Understanding of ecosystem interconnections

## Ecological Roles and Importance

### Ecosystem Services
- **Pest Control**: Controlling agricultural pests and disease vectors
- **Seed Dispersal**: Larger species spreading native plants
- **Nutrient Cycling**: Processing organic matter and prey
- **Indicator Species**: Reflecting ecosystem health

### Food Web Relationships
- Primary consumers of insects and other invertebrates
- Important prey for birds, bats, and larger reptiles
- Competitors and predators among themselves
- Links between terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems
