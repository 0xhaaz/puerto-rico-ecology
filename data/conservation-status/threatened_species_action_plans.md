# Threatened Species Action Plans for Borikén (Puerto Rico)

This document outlines comprehensive conservation action plans for Borikén's most threatened species, integrating traditional ecological knowledge with modern conservation science to create culturally respectful and scientifically sound recovery strategies.

## Framework Principles

### Integration of Knowledge Systems:
- **Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK)**: Incorporating Taíno and contemporary Boricua understanding
- **Western Science**: Using peer-reviewed research and conservation biology principles
- **Community-Based Conservation**: Centering local communities in conservation efforts
- **Adaptive Management**: Continuously adjusting strategies based on results and new knowledge

### Cultural Protocols:
- **Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC)**: Required for all conservation actions affecting traditional territories
- **Benefit Sharing**: Ensuring communities benefit from conservation efforts
- **Traditional Knowledge Protection**: Respecting sacred and sensitive knowledge
- **Community Leadership**: Supporting Indigenous-led conservation initiatives

## Critically Endangered Species

### Puerto Rican Amazon (Iguaca)
**Scientific Name**: *Amazona vittata*  
**Population**: ~500 individuals  
**Traditional Name**: Iguaca  
**Cultural Significance**: Sacred messenger bird

#### Historical Context and Traditional Knowledge:
The Iguaca was central to Taíno spiritual practices, considered a messenger between the earthly realm and the spirit world. Traditional knowledge indicates that these birds were once found throughout the island's forests and were protected through cultural taboos and sacred groves.

#### Current Threats:
1. **Habitat Loss**: Only 1% of original forest remains
2. **Hurricane Damage**: Climate change increasing storm intensity
3. **Nest Competition**: Non-native species competing for cavities
4. **Disease**: Emerging pathogens affecting captive populations
5. **Genetic Bottleneck**: Limited genetic diversity from small founding population

#### Conservation Action Plan:

**Phase 1: Immediate Stabilization (Years 1-3)**
- Expand captive breeding program with genetic diversity focus
- Establish additional breeding facilities in hurricane-resistant locations
- Create artificial nest boxes in protected areas
- Implement invasive species control in key habitats

**Phase 2: Habitat Restoration (Years 2-7)**
- Restore 5,000 hectares of native forest habitat
- Establish biological corridors connecting forest fragments
- Plant native fruiting trees important to Iguaca diet
- Create community-managed forest reserves

**Phase 3: Wild Population Enhancement (Years 5-10)**
- Release 100 individuals annually into restored habitats
- Establish satellite populations in multiple locations
- Implement long-term monitoring programs
- Develop hurricane-resistant release strategies

**Traditional Knowledge Integration:**
- Consult elders about historical Iguaca behavior and habitat preferences
- Incorporate traditional forest management practices
- Use traditional timing knowledge for optimal release periods
- Honor spiritual significance through ceremony and cultural protocols

**Community Involvement:**
- Train local youth as conservation technicians
- Develop ecotourism opportunities for sustainable income
- Create educational programs in schools and communities
- Support traditional ecological knowledge documentation

**Success Metrics:**
- Wild population reaches 1,000 individuals by year 10
- Three self-sustaining populations established
- Zero net habitat loss in range areas
- 100% community satisfaction with conservation efforts

### Puerto Rican Boa (Culebra)
**Scientific Name**: *Chilabothrus inornatus*  
**Population**: Unknown, declining  
**Traditional Name**: Majá  
**Cultural Significance**: Sacred serpent protector

#### Traditional Knowledge and Cultural Context:
In Taíno tradition, the Majá was revered as a guardian of the forest and protector of sacred sites. Traditional stories describe these serpents as wise teachers who helped maintain ecological balance. They were never killed and their presence indicated healthy forest ecosystems.

#### Current Threats:
1. **Road Mortality**: High death rates from vehicle strikes
2. **Habitat Fragmentation**: Roads and development dividing populations
3. **Introduced Predators**: Cats and mongooses killing juveniles
4. **Human Persecution**: Cultural fear leading to intentional killing
5. **Climate Change**: Altered temperature affecting reproduction

#### Conservation Action Plan:

**Phase 1: Population Assessment and Protection (Years 1-2)**
- Comprehensive population survey using radio telemetry
- Install wildlife corridors and underpasses on major roads
- Implement predator control programs in key areas
- Begin community education to reduce human persecution

**Phase 2: Habitat Management (Years 2-5)**
- Manage 50,000 hectares of boa habitat
- Create prey enhancement programs (bat conservation)
- Establish protected denning sites in caves and rock formations
- Implement fire management to maintain open forest understory

**Phase 3: Population Recovery (Years 5-15)**
- Establish captive breeding insurance population
- Create additional protected areas in key habitat
- Develop long-term research programs
- Implement genetic monitoring and management

**Traditional Knowledge Integration:**
- Map traditional sacred sites where boas were protected
- Incorporate traditional fire management practices
- Use cultural stories to educate communities about boa importance
- Consult traditional healers about historical boa ecology

**Community Programs:**
- "Boa Ambassador" education program in schools
- Community monitoring and reporting systems
- Traditional storytelling events about the Majá
- Compensation programs for landowners providing habitat

**Success Metrics:**
- Stable populations in 10 protected areas
- 50% reduction in road mortality
- Community perception shifts from fear to respect
- Traditional knowledge fully documented and integrated

## Endangered Species

### Puerto Rican Sharp-shinned Hawk (Guaraguao de Bosque)
**Scientific Name**: *Accipiter striatus venator*  
**Population**: <100 pairs  
**Traditional Name**: Guaraguao de Bosque  
**Cultural Significance**: Sky warrior and forest protector

#### Conservation Action Plan:

**Immediate Actions (Years 1-3):**
- Comprehensive population survey and nest monitoring
- Habitat protection for all known nesting areas
- Prey base assessment and enhancement
- Reduce pesticide use in hawk habitat

**Long-term Recovery (Years 3-10):**
- Restore 10,000 hectares of forest habitat
- Establish captive breeding program if needed
- Create wildlife corridors for prey movement
- Implement climate adaptation strategies

**Traditional Integration:**
- Document traditional hawk knowledge and ceremonies
- Use traditional forest management for prey habitat
- Include hawks in cultural education programs
- Establish traditional protection protocols

### Elfin-woods Warbler (Reinita de Bosque Enano)
**Scientific Name**: *Setophaga angelae*  
**Population**: 600-1,800 individuals  
**Status**: Endemic to Puerto Rico  
**Cultural Significance**: Spirit of the mountain forests

#### Conservation Action Plan:

**Habitat Conservation (Years 1-5):**
- Protect all remaining elfin woodland habitat
- Restore degraded montane forest areas
- Control invasive plant species
- Establish buffer zones around core habitat

**Climate Adaptation (Years 1-10):**
- Create climate refugia in highest elevations
- Establish seed banks for native plants
- Monitor temperature and precipitation changes
- Develop assisted migration protocols if needed

**Research and Monitoring (Ongoing):**
- Annual population surveys
- Nest monitoring and productivity assessment
- Climate impact studies
- Habitat quality monitoring
